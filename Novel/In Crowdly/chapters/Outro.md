# Outro

You have reached the end of Veronika’s story. The people who loved her had to go on without her. The children she tried to help should have had years ahead of them. No sentence at the end of a book can give those years back or make their deaths the price of an inspiring lesson.

You may have wanted Veronika to stay in Ljubljana, to finish healing, to plant trees with Eduard. She wanted to come home too. She went because she believed the people she met deserved to be heard. Both things can be true. A life is more than its last decision, and that decision does not settle how we should judge every life that ended beside hers.

The younger Veronika read the story and became angry. Then she asked for help. Years later, she stopped on a mountain path to eat with her family. Between those moments were days this book does not tell. She had to live them herself, with other people beside her. So do we all.

Whatever years you have already lived, the next ordinary day is still yours to meet. Tend a garden if you have one. Tell someone the truth. Let someone help you. Offer what help you can. You cannot know every life yours will touch, and you do not have to change the whole world to make care real in one part of it.

The book closes here. Outside it, someone is waiting to be heard. You may be that person. You may be the one who listens.
